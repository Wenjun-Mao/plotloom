## pair-1

Left: review-cb27bff2a90f5d2727e0339b398d9c30.json

Right: review-c53050fedf0e667255de5c262fa67e47.json

Overall preference: left for the story's causal and emotional construction. Right has the simpler cast, setting and viewing load, but its exclusive-delivery premise conflicts with the delivery scenes. Left still requires substantial compression for a natural 60-second presentation; this preference does not erase that weakness.

### Narrative clarity

Prefer left's underlying story. Three separately addressed letters connect a father, daughter and nurse, all alive; their incompatible claims concern one past action, while their practical requests concern whom they hope will leave. Its three endings distinguish permission to leave, companionship without reconciliation, and release from solitary duty (/storyGraph/nodes/0 and /6 through /8). Right clearly states three requests about a house in its opening cues (/sceneBeats/dialogueCues/0 through /2), but the testator, the postman's authority to receive the key, and the promised other fates are unclear. Its keep and defer endings both put luggage back inside; even the keep line is temporary (/sceneBeats/dialogueCues/5 and /7; /storyboard/shots/17 and /29). Left has more meaningful outcome separation. Right is easier to summarize at a glance, whereas left's written exposition is harder to absorb at its assigned speed.

### Branch causality

Prefer left for the final decision; neither has a strong consequential first decision. Left's first fork only changes the order of examining facts and relationships, explicitly restoring identical knowledge, letters and remaining time (/storyGraph/edges/1 and /2; /storyGraph/joinContracts/0). Right's visit and date check produce different evidence but likewise leave all ending options unchanged (/storyGraph/joinContracts/0). For the final choice, left uses separated destinations and shows whose message reaches whom, then preserves the two missed messages. Right says time allows only one delivery, yet the postman and all letters finish beside the same recipient. In the keep route, /storyboard/shots/14 delivers one letter, /18 shows two still in the bag, and /19 leaves him at that doorway because the return road is blocked. That cannot itself prevent handing over the other two. The same problem affects the other endings. Left's local evacuation paths remain contrived: its nurse can reach the daughter but is simply instructed not to carry the father's letter (/sceneBeats/beats/32 and /33). It supports the fork more clearly than right without fully motivating every restriction.

### Continuity

Prefer left's explicit knowledge and object continuity. In the nurse ending, the daughter still misunderstands her father, while the nurse gives companionship rather than unexplained knowledge (/sceneBeats/dialogueCues/7 and /8). The keeper ending leaves precisely the undelivered father-to-daughter and daughter-to-nurse originals in the bag (/storyboard/shots/44, node-c5881779-b010-505a-b41e-0fc6580c2616-s6-c1). Right also counts the remaining letters consistently, but this physical consistency exposes its core premise problem. Its house visit adds an avoidable round trip after the postman is already speaking to the recipient with the letters (/sceneBeats/beats/10 through /14). Left has more state to manage, but its supplied route descriptions manage it more deliberately.

### Performance readability and natural Chinese

Prefer left's relational exchanges, with qualifications about its explanatory voice. The daughter's recognition of permission (/sceneBeats/dialogueCues/4), the nurse's brief decision to accompany her (/6), and the keeper's short invitation to leave (/10) give actors different intentions. The paired misunderstanding and reassurance at /7 and /8 create an actual exchange, with reaction and reverse shot at /storyboard/shots/32 and /33. Right's spoken conflict setup and the recipient's weather-driven urgency are natural and concise (/sceneBeats/dialogueCues/0 through /3), but the date-check line at /4 sounds comparatively stiff and gives little personal feeling. Its keep/defer lines sound alike in intention. Left is not uniformly more natural: the repeated verdict language at /sceneBeats/dialogueCues/2 and /3 sounds analytical/legal for an intimate postal drama. Its visibleTexts sometimes describe what a letter or warning should explain rather than provide convincing letter or sign prose. Those are written presentation issues, not spoken dialogue defects.

### Shot language

Prefer left for expressiveness and staging ownership; right for production simplicity. Left assigns its people distinct spaces and contrasts lone versus paired boarding, lit versus dark duty windows, and locked clinic versus waiting companion (/storyboard/shots/22 through /25, /34, /39 through /44). The nurse/daughter reverse shot has a specific emotional job. Right's luggage and key handoff are concrete and stageable (/storyboard/shots/15, /17, /21, /23), and two recurring spaces are economical. However, its remaining-letter close-ups are asked to cover missed arrangements that exist only in beat purposes, with no visible event or supplied caption that communicates them (/sceneBeats/beats/25, /32, /39; /storyboard/shots/18, /24, /30). Left shows more of its promised consequences instead of merely labeling their absence.

### Pacing and edit cost

Prefer right for screen-time headroom and simpler assembly, independently of its causal failure. Right plans 55 seconds and 16 shots per route. Left uses exactly 60 seconds with 22 or 23 shots, five location identities and several text composites. Left's /storyboard/shots/1 through /3 allocate three seconds apiece to unfolding letters and conveying long confessions and requests. The warning at /8 has 3.5 seconds to explain postal-road versus evacuation-road rules; the nurse handoff at /29 combines delivery, reading, emotional redirection and a spoken decision in four seconds. These demands can crowd its stronger story. Right still wastes some time re-opening letters and travelling away from and back to the house. Neither plan's count alone establishes a successful edit; left's density is the more serious presentation risk.

### Separate uncertainty retained

The original assessment's objection to right's forced-delivery mechanism remains. Comparison does not authorize changing that judgment or its frozen scores. Left's comparative advantage is limited by its unmotivated refusal to use a resident as a carrier and by untested text legibility. No actual edited sequence or composite was viewed.

## pair-2

Left: review-9e8cb810a39a4bb5f7ae00f5a79bee9c.json

Right: review-2dbaa6c9c8fc3d4c6e0dd064ae772c1a.json

Overall preference: right for a more observable mystery, more distinct endings and better retained branch state. Left is substantially leaner and leaves more room for quiet performance. Right's stronger construction comes with more shots, conditional ending variants and unresolved motivation around bell surrender.

### Narrative clarity

Prefer right. Its letter makes a specific prediction, spoken aloud at /sceneBeats/dialogueCues/2 and realized by the post breaking at /storyboard/shots/11 or /19. The audience can observe why the letter matters. Left asserts recognition of a future-self signature (/sceneBeats/beats/1) but provides no actual letter wording there; its eventual blank-paper revelation lacks a prior expectation that makes the blank surprising (/storyboard/shots/14). Right's return, leave and send-back outcomes enact different relationships to identity and the letter (/storyGraph/nodes/6 through /8). Left's final staying line is emotionally clear, but the costs of surrendering or crossing remain thin. Right still does not make the original identity legible or explain the time mechanism; those remain mysteries rather than demonstrated answers.

### Branch causality

Prefer right, boundedly. Left's first choice discovers either matching marks or an acoustic gap, then reconverges without changing the final action or consequence (/storyGraph/joinContracts/0). A route that never probes the gap still crosses successfully. Right's first choice actually relocates the bell and changes the later sound source and leave action (/storyGraph/edges/1 and /2; /storyboard/shots/33). Its final letter decisions are more consequential: retain it and return to confusion, destroy it and leave, or send it into the apparent loop. But the price-of-passage premise remains weak when keeping the bell allows the same easy crossing (/storyGraph/nodes/0 through /3). Neither sample earns a causal relationship between bell handling and the mystery's underlying cause; right at least preserves an observable consequence of handling it.

### Continuity

Prefer right. The join unifies place, time, intact letter and fulfilled prediction while preserving shore-bell versus boat-bell state (/storyGraph/joinContracts/0). Common ending shot descriptions explicitly select only the incoming state; the leave beat presses a bell only when it is present (/storyboard/shots/23, /24, /33). Letter tears, folds and corner defect are tracked, and the flashback is marked as past rather than a new transfer (/storyboard/shots/29 through /31, /37 through /41). Left's simple object chain is mostly understandable, but its join does not carry forward the different discoveries in any meaningful way, and surrender becomes leaving the bell ashore without a recipient (/storyboard/shots/10). Right's textual continuity is stronger; no rendered conditional variant has been inspected, so that is not a claim of implementation success.

### Performance readability and natural Chinese

Mixed, with right stronger at decisive moments and left retaining a useful conversational softness. Right's short identity question, letter address and prediction create a clear sequence of bewilderment, reading and watchfulness (/sceneBeats/dialogueCues/0 through /2). Its repeated identity question in the return ending is a meaningful performance callback (/6). Left's mark-confirmation line at /2 and final self-directed staying line at /5 are intelligible and less compressed than right's abbreviated return intention at /5. Right's short acknowledgement after the post breaks (/3 and /4) is easy to say, but the implied disappointment is not fully prepared: why should moving the bell prevent the post from breaking? The send-back recognition at /8 supplies an actor's emotional landing while the image provides only suggestive evidence. Neither brevity nor longer phrasing is automatically more natural; the line's implied thought must be supported.

### Shot language

Prefer right for its central proof and matched-object payoff; left for a few economical images. Right establishes the intact post, fixes its grain and tide line, then shows the break on the predicted light cue (/storyboard/shots/4, /11, /19). Reusing the letter pickup at /40 gives its final revelation a visual rhyme. Left's five-second extreme close-up comparing the missing marks (/storyboard/shots/5, node-72aeeb34-2b89-52a9-bc80-2492f737e5aa-s1-c2) is a good compact clue. Its echo close-up (/8) does not show why a returning sound identifies a navigable gap, and its medium landing shot (/14) must carry an important paper detail without an isolating insert. Right supplies more deliberate spatial and prop anchors, although sound remains largely prose in empty audio plans in both samples.

### Pacing and edit cost

Prefer left for economy. Its six routes use 42 or 44 seconds and ten shots each; right uses 57 or 60 seconds and 20 to 22 shots. Left can hold a face, mark comparison or dawn image rather than repeatedly cutting through small hand actions. Right spends separate shots tearing the letter, releasing each half, speaking, handling or hearing the bell, rowing and receding (/storyboard/shots/29 through /35). This can articulate relinquishment but is also an avoidable assembly burden within one minute. Preserving the two bell states in shared endings further increases variant work; the send-back ending adds reverse-flow compositing and a flashback. Left's simpler plan does not compensate for weaker stakes, and its crossing/landing shots also combine several actions, but its pace has appreciably more room.

### Separate uncertainty retained

Right's mysterious send-back action is not fully motivated by a discoverable clue, and its flashback shows the letter beside the bell rather than arriving from water (/sceneBeats/beats/42 through /45; /storyboard/shots/40). The pair comparison supports a relative preference without treating the time loop as explained or altering the frozen continuity judgment. The bell surrender rule is unresolved in both samples.

## pair-3

Left: review-4a69cf3be04f094eb5592f28e2b547d1.json

Right: review-2ac95d75a0fd7f53f5b480d512f1ded8.json

Overall preference: qualified left for personalizing memory loss, retaining its irreversibility and staging the actual supply outcomes. Right is shorter, sometimes more economical in speech, and gives a useful family-photograph test. Both leave the theater-versus-hospital choice dramatically under-motivated, so left is not a fully resolved ethical drama.

### Narrative clarity

Mixed; prefer left's personal through-line and right's simpler immediate statement of the situation. Left's resident can physically open the door but cannot recognize home (/sceneBeats/beats/1 through /3; /sceneBeats/dialogueCues/0). It later makes clear that darkness does not return memory and refusal prevents new harm without fixing prior harm (/storyGraph/nodes/3 and /8). Right's opening switch/light/family sequence states the premise quickly (/sceneBeats/beats/0 through /3). Its photo exchange is a concrete confirmation rather than an abstract report (/storyboard/shots/5). Left's repeated confirmations are clearer about irreversibility but slower. Neither supplies a particular person, promise or community need explaining why the theater merits the final power; left explicitly shows an empty stage (/storyboard/shots/26), making that motivational gap especially visible.

### Branch causality

Mixed, with left giving more complete final consequences and neither providing strong first-choice agency. Both investigate either records or people, return to the same controls and offer all final options (/storyGraph/joinContracts/0 in each). Left's resident route shows loss persisting as light fades (/sceneBeats/beats/20 and /21), whereas its records route does not independently demonstrate that conclusion before the join. Right's visit route observes one family but is treated as confirming the universal rule; the records branch has the stronger basis for that inference. Left's final supply endings show the chosen venue, the unchosen venue and the asserted new loss (/storyboard/shots/19 through /23 and /26 through /30). Right's hospital and theater endings mainly substitute venue labels and use the same family reaction. Left's causal accounting is fuller, but its new victim is unseen and certified only by the electrician's voice-over (/sceneBeats/dialogueCues/7 and /9).

### Continuity

Prefer left. The same resident, key, threshold and cabinet recur, and no branch test secretly spends power. Left distinguishes expended power after supply from unused low power after refusal (/storyboard/shots/18, /25, /31). The refusal ending preserves the original resident's loss and keeps the second household door closed (/storyboard/shots/32 and /35). Right preserves the basic switching rule but introduces a household's photograph and changing memory-loss information with little persistent identity or source definition (/sceneBeats/beats/9, /22, /34). Both conveniently obtain current memory-loss reports without showing who reports them; left's immediate current report is particularly conspicuous because the resident has only just discovered her confusion. Better tracking of objects does not resolve that informational gap.

### Performance readability and natural Chinese

Prefer left for the two-person encounter, with mixed results in the moral commentary. Its question invites the resident to search, and her answer separates the familiar key from the unfamiliar interior (/sceneBeats/dialogueCues/5 and /6). This gives both performers an objective and a response; the 5.5-second close-up at /storyboard/shots/13, node-46afee8a-7967-50b4-bb64-357e500ef48f-s1-c2, has room for hesitation. Right's family silently exchanging a photograph is also playable and avoids overexplaining the loss. Right's pattern-confirmation line (/sceneBeats/dialogueCues/2) is naturally elliptical in context, but its supply-ending lines (/3 and /4) simply report the destination rather than reveal a relationship or difficult thought. Left's refusal line (/12) takes explicit responsibility, yet its hospital ending's metaphorical acknowledgement (/8) sounds crafted rather than spontaneous. Its theater ending statement (/10) answers an expectation of recovered memory that the story has not clearly established. Left's more specific writing is not uniformly more idiomatic.

### Shot language

Prefer left for spatial coherence and independently visible venue results; retain right's family scene as a strong local example. Left fixes cabinet, near doorway and distant doorway on one slope, uses the key near the resident's face, and cuts to a hospital corridor or empty stage with consistent geometry (/storyBible/locations/0 through /2; /storyboard/shots/13, /19, /26). Right puts the destination on a station display, which is economical (/storyboard/shots/12 and /16). However, its /13 and /17 combine a different household at its doorway with the electrician at the station in one medium shot without specifying a monitor, cut or other spatial bridge. Left avoids that ambiguity by keeping the new doorway in the same slope shot (/21 and /28), but an empty opening door is weaker evidence of memory loss than right's intended family disorientation. Clearer staging and stronger evidence are distinct strengths here.

### Pacing and edit cost

Prefer right for duration and cut economy, with qualifications. Right uses 45 or 46 seconds and 12 shots per route; left uses 57 or 59 seconds and 19 shots. Left spends more time checking records, repeating the key/threshold denial, observing dimming, returning to controls and stating the final burden. It also needs separate hospital/theater images and stable lighting variants. Right's station displays avoid some location work, but the composite or edit solution for /storyboard/shots/13 and /17 remains unspecified; its smaller shot count understates that decision. Neither has been rendered or timed in performance. Left uses its extra work to make some outcomes more concrete, but not all of the additional repetition earns its screen time.

### Separate uncertainty retained

The comparative preference concerns the supplied text and shot plan, not whether an audience will feel the intended moral dilemma. In both samples the theater rationale and the source of memory-loss reports remain unprovided. Left's empty-door/voice-over treatment is clearer to stage than right's combined medium shot but does not constitute stronger direct observation of the new household's condition. These distinctions are recorded without changing any initial score.
