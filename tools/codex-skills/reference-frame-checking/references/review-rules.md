# Review rules

Inspect the actual still at usable resolution alongside relevant references. Assess the visible subject's identity and costume, the specified beginning state versus a completed action, camera/framing, the visible world, hands and prop ownership, and visible readable text or implausible money. Use source context to interpret the cut, not as a demand to show the entire world in every frame. Judge picture text by what is actually readable and required; do not invent exact words. A single still cannot prove motion, future action, dialogue, unseen anatomy or hidden-state continuity.

A material contradiction must be observable, source-supported and relevant to the frame's intended role. An endpoint already reached can matter when it prevents the planned beginning action. A hidden or cropped prop, person, or mouth is not automatically a contradiction; a small framing difference or ambiguous hand is not a material failure without evidence of consequence. Explain unresolved necessary identity or state as uncertainty, not a defect. Missing context or image access prevents a supported pass; an independently observed material contradiction can still support failure.

Use `pass` only when all supplied frames and references were actually viewed and no material contradiction or unresolved required issue remains. Use `fail` for an observed material contradiction supported by viewed evidence; use `needs_review` for unresolved required evidence when no independently observed material failure exists. Record modest variation as minor. These are recommendations, not creative or owner acceptance.
