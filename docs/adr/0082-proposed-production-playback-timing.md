# ADR 0082 (proposed): Authored shot time versus reviewed playback time

**Status:** Accepted for the bounded reviewed-playback-segment implementation, 2026-09-23. This authorizes synthetic-media contract work and an explicit creator-reviewed local derivative, not provider generation, source retiming, or creative/media acceptance. The original proposal text below remains the decision basis; its former open product choices are settled by the approval noted under “First slice.”

## Problem and evidence

The [U4 handoff receipt](../verification/2026-09-23-u4-shot-handoff-readiness.md) retains 26 six-second F5 cuts and one eight-second cut. The [bridge](0079-f5-production-canonical-bridge.md) installs their exact source coordinates and milliseconds into canonical shots; [trusted timing](0018-trusted-story-timing-allocation.md) makes authored time the story authority. No U4 shot has an approved/selectable production take. Plotloom admits H3 requests of 5 or 8 seconds, not 6 ([ADR 0066](0066-h3-qualified-job-duration-contract.md)); bridge video preparation currently insists that the request equal its source cut. The gateway's 24-fps `17k + 5` grid implies 124, 158, and 192 *requested* frames for nominal 5, 6, and 8 seconds. Only 5/8 outputs were physically measured; there is no measured six-second output. A nominal six-second request would imply 158 frames (about 6.583 seconds), not six seconds.

The current media job freezes a request and source shot, then probes an ingested MP4's format duration, frame rate/count and codecs. H3 checks its frozen requested frame count, allowing one frame of container-duration tolerance. It does **not** prove equality with the authored shot length. `VideoCandidateSelectionRow` selects a whole job, with no in/out coordinates; the route players stream that whole file and advance on native `ended`. `timeline.py` still sums authored `ShotV2.durationUnits`, so a longer file can make actual route playback diverge from its timecode. The current media endpoint serves only the original job bytes. No stitching/export pipeline presently corrects this. Native AAC is part of the delivered take, not evidence that dialogue or sound ends at an authored cut.

## Proposed ownership and contract

| Value | Owner | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| F5 source cut and canonical shot duration (integer milliseconds) | Accepted/current bridge and canonical Storyboard; canonical timeline | Story duration and ordering. Technical bridge acceptance is not human creative or Storyboard Approval. Never inferred from a provider request or a file. |
| Backend requested seconds, profile, fps, expected frames | Frozen video job and qualified adapter | An execution choice/capacity reservation, not the playback length. An 8-second request may intentionally seek a six-second authored segment only after this policy is approved. |
| Actual decoded frames, presentation timestamps, audio coverage and container metadata | Trusted probe of the immutable output bytes | Measured candidate evidence. The present `format.duration` float and `nb_frames` alone are insufficient for a frame-exact segment decision. |
| Playback segment and playable derivative | Explicit author/reviewer decision bound to a current candidate and canonical shot | The only route-playback unit. Neither ingest nor whole-take selection silently makes a longer take fit a shorter shot. |

For the first production slice, offer a bounded **contiguous frame window** from a currently ingested, fully decodable, qualified H3 take. Use half-open `[inFrame, outFrame)` coordinates in the take's decoded/presentation order and its rational 24-fps timebase, not decimal seconds or browser `currentTime` as authority. The selected window must contain exactly `shot.durationUnits × fps / 1000` frames when that quotient is an integer. Thus a U4 six-second shot needs 144 presented frames; an eight-second shot needs 192. If a shot duration is not representable on this grid, block and ask for an explicit timing/product decision; do not round or alter the shot. Whole-take use is an explicit window too, not a special exemption. A five-second H3 take's expected 124 frames cannot supply a six-second window. An eight-second take's expected 192 frames *could* supply one, but quality and useful in/out placement are unproven.

At preparation, replace the bridge's present request-equals-source rule only with a narrow, explicit *segment-required* intent: freeze the accepted bridge admission/revision, cut ID and exact source coordinates/hash/milliseconds, canonical shot/revision/duration, Storyboard Approval and keyframe lineage, plus the qualified 8-second request; display the duration difference before dispatch and require enough requested frames for the source window. Revalidate that binding at prepare, segment selection, playable-manifest construction and selected-derivative serve time. Keep the existing exact-match path for an eight-second source; do not introduce a blanket mismatch bypass or mark a mismatched job playable. The intent is a request choice, not authority to cut or select its result.

Keep the original ingested take and its immutable digest. A reviewable segment proposal binds the same exact source/currentness evidence as preparation, job ID, frozen request/hash, output hash, probe evidence, `[inFrame, outFrame)`, native-audio treatment, and expected selection revision. Construct a local, non-destructive, deterministic H.264/AAC playback derivative from the chosen frames and matching audio interval; persist its digest and a post-derivation decoded-frame/PTS/audio probe. The full-take player stays available for comparison. The author must inspect **the final segment with sound** (including start/end, any clipped speech/action, subtitles, transitions and sound tail), then explicitly select the candidate **and** segment in one revision-checked review transaction. Reject/regenerate or choose another window if cutting removes required dialogue/action or creates an audible/visual discontinuity. No automatic acceptance because arithmetic fits. Candidate selection and segment revision share the existing per-shot selection owner; a changed take, output digest, window, derivative, source timing/Approval, or review invalidates the playable decision. A later explicit rejection clears a selected segment without erasing evidence. The legacy whole-job review endpoint cannot select an H3 take under this contract. Portable snapshots must include proposed as well as selected derivative bytes, and a take with retained proposals cannot be discarded; the original and derivative remain available for comparison. Timing review does not qualify F6 audiovisual content: its two retained, unselected clips stay rejected/unselected, and any future production selection additionally needs an explicit audiovisual-quality decision; current `job.current` alone is not that gate.

The media-serving contract should give route players only the verified selected derivative (or a verified whole take whose explicit window is physically exact); the original job-media URL remains the full-take review surface. Both ordered and branching playback must use the same selection projection, include segment/derivative identity in their reset key, and block missing/stale/unverified segments. Native browser seeking or `timeupdate` cannot be the sole cut boundary: event cadence and seek precision do not prove frame-accurate presentation. The canonical scene/path timecode remains authored; the playable manifest must assert each selected unit has the same frame-representable duration before claiming a complete route. Future export/stitching, if added, must consume this same reviewed manifest and derivatives, not full original takes or nominal requests. Audio cannot be independently padded, sped up, or dropped to make a mismatch disappear.

Existing whole-job selections without a segment decision remain review evidence, not automatically migrated playable selections; the first implementation requires explicit re-review under the new contract. It must not rewrite retained F6/U4 media or imply old reviews approved a cut they never saw.

For this first slice, require source video to probe as presentation-order constant-frame-rate 24/1 with consecutive frame timestamps; reject variable/uncertain cadence rather than infer from `avg_frame_rate`. Let `t0` be the first presented video-frame PTS on the common media timeline. Decode/re-encode arbitrary windows (no keyframe-dependent stream copy) and normalize derivative video PTS to `0, 1/24, …, (N−1)/24`, with exactly `N` frames and an `N/24` presentation interval. Select audio by presentation time `[t0 + inFrame/24, t0 + outFrame/24)`; map each boundary to the decoded PCM sample grid relative to the audio stream's own PTS origin using ceiling, require source audio coverage of the full interval, and normalize audible output to zero without stretch, filler, or a sample outside that interval. AAC priming/padding may affect encoded packets or container duration but must be accounted for by edit metadata or verified decoding: decoded presented audio may differ from the rational boundary by at most one sample, with no audible pre-roll/tail. A missing/extra presented video frame, coverage gap, unaccounted timestamp offset, or larger audio difference blocks. Compare counted frames, video/audio PTS, stream and `format.duration`; disagreements outside these exact rules make timing uncertain, not “close enough.” Do not treat H3's one-frame *ingestion* tolerance as a one-frame *playback* allowance.

## First slice, alternatives and decision boundary

Approved first implementation: build the probe/segment/selection contract and minimal Chinese review UI for current 24-fps H3 6/8-second authored shots; use qualified 8-second requests as possible source takes for six-second windows, while preserving exact source timing and all existing Approval/reference/keyframe, dispatch and quality gates. The creator may choose **any contiguous exact-duration in/out window** with its sound, inspect the final derivative, and explicitly confirm the selected take/window; the system must not claim that a human watched it merely because a control was clicked. Show separately “原稿镜头时长”, “后端请求时长”, “实测原片”, and “已审核播放片段”; label an 8-second request as *candidate material for a six-second segment*, not as an exact match. Block with a clear reason for no Approval, stale source/selection, no qualified request, missing frames/audio, non-frame-aligned shot, unreviewed window, mismatched probe, or clipped creative content. A six-second catalog entry must not be introduced on the assumption that `158/24` equals six.

- Reauthor F5/canonical shots to 5/8 seconds: simplest mechanically, but changes accepted story pacing and may invalidate source bindings, dialogue budgets, approvals, and 26 U4 cuts. Requires a distinct author creative decision; never automatic.
- Qualify a nominal 6-second H3 request: still predicts 158 frames, so it does not itself solve exact six-second playback. Needs measured output and then the same explicit playback policy.
- Find another qualified exact-duration provider: could avoid a derivative, but adds provider/quality/cost authority and cannot be presumed here.
- Browser-only in/out or silent post-ingest trim: smaller code, but fragile at frame/audio boundaries or hides an editorial choice; rejected as the production authority. A local derived clip is a constrained playback artifact, not permission for general editing or a change to the original take.

This decision narrowly supersedes ADR 0079's bridge request-equals-source admission rule for a frozen segment-required request. It clarifies ADR 0066's no-trim rule: never trim or tolerate a wrong H3 *output* to pass ingestion, but permit a separately proven and reviewed derivative of a fully valid original take as a *playback* unit. Original H3 request/output checks remain intact; there is no generic duration-mismatch bypass. This does not reopen failed F6 dialogue quality or authorize a third live submission. The creator owns the arbitrary contiguous in/out choice and explicit selected-segment confirmation; this is not automatic human creative approval of the story or a rejected F6 take.

## 2026-09-23 review-workbench clarification

For personal playback-take and segment review, the explicit select/reject action and its revision-checked target are the decision; reviewer name and note are optional annotations, not gates or evidence that a named person watched the media. The API accepts omitted, empty, or one-character annotations, trims supplied text, and stores absence as empty strings in the existing non-null review columns. It never invents an identity or reason. Existing nonempty annotations remain unchanged. This changes only review metadata, not the requirement to explicitly select a current, verified playback derivative or the rejection/CAS/currentness rules above. A clear Chinese error must explain failed or stale selection rather than expose a generic validation status. The workbench shows original take, proposed segment, and selected segment as distinct states; technical IDs, revisions, and frozen request details remain available in a disclosure rather than dominating the review path. Supporting keyframe and reference work stays mounted behind disclosures so local drafts and deep links survive navigation. Storyboard Approval remains a separate append-only, exact-revision gate whose user-supplied local reviewer label is required; its form is folded by default but the approval and revocation controls remain explicit and unchanged.

## Verification required before product acceptance


Use deterministic synthetic H.264/AAC media with known frame numbers, PTS, spoken/audio markers and subtitle/end markers: 124/144/158/192-frame cases; exact six/eight windows at different in-points; short output; long output; nonzero PTS; variable/missing `nb_frames`; wrong stream/container duration; off-grid authored milliseconds; AAC delay/tail; corrupt or changed hash. Assert no row or dispatch on invalid preparation, no implicit selection from ingest, CAS/currentness across source/bridge/Storyboard/Approval/keyframe edit and reselect/reopen/restart, and that neither ordered nor branching playback mounts or advances a partial, stale or unverified route manifest. Verify derivative frame sequence, sound boundaries and hashes with FFmpeg/ffprobe; a browser walkthrough checks controls and honest Chinese states, not frame precision by itself. A separately bounded, explicitly authorized live canary may follow only after implementation proof and product review. Do not generate media in this implementation pass.
