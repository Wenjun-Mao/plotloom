# ADR 0236: Task-owned local QA cleanup

## Status

Accepted 2026-11-06 after the owner approved the
[reviewed cleanup plan](../roadmap/2026-11-06-local-qa-storage-cleanup.md).

## Context

Closed QA work accumulated copied environments, raw frames, synthetic tensors,
transport archives and duplicated project fixtures. Historical evidence folders
also acquired a few real current dependencies. Directory age and prior retention
wording cannot distinguish disposable payloads from those dependencies.

## Decision

The producing task owns ordinary authorized scratch cleanup as part of its
known-terminal closeout, including failed attempts. Before creating durable
output, identify its working directory, deliverable and cleanup owner in the
existing plan. At adoption or supersession, release obsolete captures, transport
copies and environments after their consumers no longer need them.

Retain an item only for a named current function, owner-valued work or necessary
recovery. Each exception records its path, consumer, deletion consequence,
owner and release event in the existing plan. The repository coordinator reviews
sizes and unresolved exceptions at the next weekly maintenance session. Age
prompts review, not automatic deletion. No timer, daemon or blanket deletion
authority is created. Generation budgets and provider authority do not change.

Deletion of an approved historical batch binds exact files or unchanged subtrees
to a fresh no-follow manifest. Revalidate identity, membership, current consumers,
open handles and lifecycle ownership; defer new or changed children. Protect the
dependency closure actually used by active work, not whole neighboring studies.
Use native project deletion/abandonment where that lifecycle owns the target.
Do not clear live UNKNOWN reservations, manufacture settlement, retry old work,
or bootstrap obsolete runtime state to make it deletable.

Keep useful current contracts, decisions and truthful pass/fail/UNKNOWN outcomes
in their owning documents. Retire stale artifact-availability links when their
files are removed. A frozen receipt does not require perpetual retention of
every raw payload; retained immutable evidence is not rewritten to imply a
different outcome. Keep one usable current recovery package; current product
assets and snapshot/restore remain protected under their own contracts.

## Consequences

Cleanup is a task completion responsibility, with scoped preservation readback.
It is not a production generation step or a reason to repeat full qualification
for storage-only work. Project-owned handoff packages retain their lifecycle;
there is no independent TTL for their `delivery` subdirectories. Gateway-managed
transfer retention and verified ImageGen staging cleanup keep their existing
contracts. The procedure creates no new evidence library or cleanup framework.
